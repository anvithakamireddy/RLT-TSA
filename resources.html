WHAT TO WEAR

Dress Code

If you compete without proper competition attire, you lose 20% of the points for that round.

Competition Attire

  • Official royal blue TSA shirt
  • Gray pants, or a gray skirt at least knee-length
  • Black dress shoes with black or dark blue socks
  • Not allowed: jeans, leggings, shorts, athletic shoes, or boots

Chapter Team (additional)

  • Navy blazer with the official TSA patch
  • Official TSA tie (required for males; anyone may wear it)
  • Anyone can wear this in other events too

General Sessions

  • TSA shirt preferred, or a button-down or polo
  • Pants, or a dress or skirt at least knee-length
  • Dress shoes; open-toed shoes are acceptable
  • Casual clothes are only OK outside competitions and sessions
03
KNOW THE RULES

Guidelines & Rules

These rules apply to every event, on top of each event's own rules.

Membership & Registration+
  • You and your chapter must be affiliated with TSA to compete.
  • You can enter up to 6 events per conference, counting individual and team events together.
  • Every team member must be from the same chapter. Teams have 6 members max unless the event says otherwise.
  • For events with online submissions, the advisor picks one team captain to upload the entry.
  • 9th graders compete at the level their chapter is affiliated with.
Your Entry+
  • Start and finish your entry during the current school year. Each entry can only be used for one year and one competition.
  • One entry per person or team per event. All entries must be in English.
  • Put only your participant or team ID on your entry, with no names, school, or state (unless the event says otherwise).
  • Displays can't be larger than 15" deep x 2.5' wide x 4' high unless the event says otherwise.
  • Portfolios turned in on-site go in a clear front report cover.
  • Check in and pick up your entry at the times listed in the conference program.
Citations & Copyright+
  • Cite all ideas, text, images, and sounds from other sources, even ones labeled "free to reuse".
  • Use a professional citation style such as MLA, APA, Chicago, or IEEE. Not doing so costs 20%.
  • If you use copyrighted material, include written permission.
  • Many events require the Student Copyright Checklist, so check your event's rules.
Honor Statement & AI+
  • All work must be created by you or your team.
  • Generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, etc.) are not allowed unless your event specifically calls for them.
  • Plagiarism, cheating, and falsifying information are prohibited.
  • A violation costs 20%.
Prohibited Content & Materials+
  • No hazardous materials, chemicals, open flames, or wet-cell batteries.
  • No images of guns, knives, or other weapons.
  • No slurs, gang references, or vulgar, violent, or suggestive content.
  • No promoting products you can't legally buy, such as tobacco, alcohol, or drugs.
  • Video and game content should fit the ESRB "E for Everyone" guidance.
Judging, Deductions & Rounds+
  • Every rubric starts with Go/No Go specifications. If any is missing, your entry isn't judged at all.
  • A rules violation that gives you an unfair advantage costs 20% of the round's points, or you may be disqualified.
  • Events with a semifinal take at least 12 semifinalists. The top 10 finalists are announced at the awards ceremony.
  • Judges' decisions are final. Only state advisors can file a grievance with the Rules Interpretation Panel.
Devices & What to Bring+
  • Phones, smartwatches, and other devices must be off during competition unless your event allows them.
  • Tests are online, so bring a laptop or tablet that runs 2 hours on battery. Chrome is preferred.
  • You may bring a mouse, but external keyboards and monitors aren't allowed.
  • If you need pencils, bring two sharpened #2 or mechanical pencils with erasers.
  • You're responsible for making sure any websites or online files open on your own device.

PUT IT TOGETHER

Portfolio Tips

  1. Follow the page order exactly. Every event lists its required pages in order, and the rubric checks it.
  2. Title page. Event title, your participant or team ID, the conference city and state, and the year.
  3. Printed portfolios. Single-sided 8½" x 11" pages in a clear front report cover.
  4. Digital portfolios. One multi-page PDF. Make sure links open without asking for access.
  5. Page limits. Stay within the limit listed for each section, such as "one page" or "maximum of two pages".
  6. Citations. Cite as you go in a professional style. Purdue OWL MLA guide ↗
